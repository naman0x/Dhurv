01 / WHY FOOD HYGIENE MATTERS

FOOD SAFETY IS A
CHAIN. ONE WEAK
STEP CREATES RISK.

From farm and market to kitchen counter and dining plate, food passes through five critical stages. A single lapse in hand hygiene, water quality, or storage temperature compromises the entire chain.

THE 5-STAGE FOOD SAFETY CHAIN
01 Preparation → 02 Cooking → 03 Storage → 04 Serving → 05 Consumption
FARM TO PLATE PROTECTION

Strong habits at every stage — washing, cooking, chilling, and covering — keep the entire chain safe.

🦠 01
CONTAMINATION
Harmful biological, chemical or physical agents can enter food at any stage.
🤢 02
FOODBORNE ILLNESS
Unsafe food can cause vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain and fever.
🏠 03
EVERYDAY RISK
Food safety matters at home, school, restaurants and street-food stalls.
❤️ 04
PREVENTION
Most critical preventive actions are simple, consistent daily habits.
TRANSMISSION FLOW

Source → Food → Human → Health Risk: Breaking the chain through clean water, covering food, and temperature control protects health.

02 / CONTAMINATION VECTORS

THREE WAYS FOOD
BECOMES UNSAFE.

Breaking the transmission flow (Source → Food → Human → Health Risk) requires targeting all three classes of food contamination:

🦠

01 · BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINATION

Bacteria, viruses and parasites — the leading cause of foodborne illness worldwide.

Prevention: Cook thoroughly, keep clean, separate raw & cooked, chill promptly.

🧪

02 · CHEMICAL CONTAMINATION

Pesticide residues, unwashed produce, unsafe water, or leached container toxins.

Prevention: Use clean water, rinse produce under running water, use food-grade storage.

🧱

03 · PHYSICAL CONTAMINATION

Dust, hair, insects, or foreign fragments entering uncovered meals.

Prevention: Keep food covered with lids/nets and inspect ingredients before cooking.

RULE 01 / WHO FIVE KEYS

KEEP CLEAN

Why: Wash hands and keep surfaces and utensils clean so microbes don't transfer to food.

Example: Wash hands with soap for 20s before preparing food and after handling raw meat.

Daily Action: Make handwashing a fixed routine before every meal and cooking session.

CROSS-CONTAMINATION PREVENTION
🍗 Raw→🔪 Knife→🪵 Board→🍲 Meal

SEPARATE RAW & COOKED

Use dedicated cutting boards and store raw meat below cooked food in the fridge.

05
DIAGNOSTIC ANALYSIS

Two Major Problems Identified & Common Mistakes in the Data

PROBLEM 01 KNOWLEDGE DEFICIT
Lack of Awareness

Some critical food-safety habits are still not widely recognized or practiced. In our survey, practices requiring microbiological or label awareness showed the lowest consistent adoption:

  • Separating raw and cooked food: Followed consistently by only 43.6% (24 of 55).
  • Considering hygiene while eating outside: Followed consistently by only 49.1% (27 of 55).
  • Checking expiry / use-by information: Followed consistently by only 52.7% (29 of 55).
PROBLEM 02 BEHAVIORAL GAP
Inconsistent Hygiene Practices

Even when people know a food-hygiene rule matters, many follow it only “Sometimes” rather than every day — creating recurring exposure risks:

  • Highest “Sometimes” rate: Separating raw and cooked food is practiced only sometimes by 30.9% (17 respondents).
  • Outside food & expiry checks: 29.1% and 27.3% follow these habits only occasionally.
  • Consistency threshold: 4 out of 8 practices are followed consistently by fewer than 60% of respondents.

“The survey provides a data-based view of everyday food and hygiene habits among 55 respondents. Way Forward — 8 Practical Recommendations
01Wash hands regularly with soap & clean water
02Wash fruits and vegetables thoroughly under running water
03Keep cooked and stored food covered at all times
04Separate raw and cooked food using distinct boards/containers
05Store perishable food safely below 5°C promptly
06Check expiry / use-by information before purchase & use
07Maintain clean utensils, knives, and kitchen surfaces

PROJECT TITLE Food and Hygiene Habits Amongst People: Survey, Analysis & Awareness Study
RESEARCH DESIGN Primary Questionnaire Survey (n = 55) + Digital Intervention Platform
FOCAL AREA Public Health, Nutrition, Clean Water & Food Hygiene

1. Research Objectives & Methodology

To evaluate everyday food-hygiene practices across five age cohorts (n = 55: 13–17 yrs 25.5%, 18–25 yrs 29.1%, 26–40 yrs 23.6%, 41–60 yrs 16.4%, 60+ yrs 5.5%) and identify the gap between general hygiene awareness and consistent daily execution.

2. Two Major Problems Identified

  • Problem 01 — Lack of Awareness: Critical invisible-risk practices have low consistent adoption: Separating raw & cooked food (43.6%), Hygiene while eating outside (49.1%), and Checking expiry dates (52.7%).
  • Problem 02 — Inconsistent Hygiene Practices: Up to 30.9% follow key habits only “Sometimes”, leaving 4 of 8 core habits below the 60% consistency mark.

1. Why Food Hygiene Matters Globally & Locally

According to the WHO, over 866 million foodborne illnesses and 1.52 million deaths occur globally every year. Yet foodborne disease is almost entirely preventable—because food safety is a 5-stage chain (Preparation → Cooking → Storage → Serving → Consumption) driven by everyday household habits.
